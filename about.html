About This Project

My name is Wout Neutkens. I am an independent thinker, interested in the foundations of reality: from computational physics, theories of consciousness (IIT), and finitistic mathematics to the moral question of how we can eliminate suffering and stack happiness.

The essay, The Cathedral of Cause and Experience, is the first stone. It postulates a single, reliable rule as the foundation of everything. From that follows a path toward sufficient safety and an infinite stacking of happiness — not as a utopia, but as a technical and moral project.

On the Blog, reflections, additions, and insights appear regularly.

📖 A Reading Plan

The essay is coherent, but you can enter it through different doors. Here are three routes, depending on what you're looking for.

Route 1: The Quick Round (20–30 min)

Understand the core idea: a reliable foundation for infinite happiness.

  • Read "The Productive Refusal". This introduces the three pillars and the power of saying "no" to completed infinities.
  • Then read "Getting Used to What Never Gets Boring". Happiness is not a destination but a skill: becoming attuned to inexhaustible novelty. Familiarity deepens joy when the underlying rule guarantees endless new patterns to integrate.
  • Optional, for a short inspirational finish: read the final chapter "Constructing the Neverending Paradise" — the vision of Heaven as a direction, not a destination.

Route 2: The Foundations Route (45–60 min)

For those who ask: Why believe this? What is the evidence? How certain can we be?

  • Step 1 — The logical bedrock: Read the blog post Finitism and the Origin. Why an infinite past is incoherent, and why a single beginning is not a leap of faith but a subtraction of absurdity.
  • Step 2 — Why this rule, not any other: Read the essay chapter "Why One Rule Beats All". The attack on the Ruliad (the "everything exists" multiverse) and the restoration of causality and explanatory power.
  • Step 3 — The evidence that it works: Read "The Scientific Path to the Spiritual Goal". Smallpox, polio, and how entire categories of suffering have already been closed. These are existence proofs that the project is not fantasy but a continuation of what we have already begun.
  • Step 4 — The nature of our certainty: Read the blog post The Battle for Certainty. We cannot have absolute certainty — but we can build a probability that asymptotically approaches it. The question is not whether Heaven is guaranteed, but whether the path is reliable enough to bet on.

Route 3: The Experience Route (60+ min)

For those who ask: What does this mean for me, my happiness, and my choices?

  • First read the blog post The Whisper of the Ordinary. This is about the pitfall of habituation: can we truly be happy, or do we just get used to our prison?
  • Then read in the essay: "Where the Rule Becomes Feeling" and "Two Conditions of One System". Here the link is made between physics (Φ) and the biological origin of suffering and happiness.
  • Finally, read the blog post How Big is Big? Not for the numbers, but for the philosophy: the universe is finite, but the possibilities are so overwhelmingly large (TREE(3), the Busy Beaver) that we will never be bored. An infinite future of genuine novelty is possible.

Which route suits you?

  • Are you an optimist? Start with Route 1.
  • Are you a skeptic? Start with Route 2.
  • Are you driven by experience? Take Route 3.

All parts together form the cathedral. The reading plan is just the door that fits you best. Enjoy the read.
