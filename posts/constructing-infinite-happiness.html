Constructing Infinite Happiness

26 June 2026

The essays and posts on this site orbit a single question: can happiness be built without a ceiling? The Cathedral of Cause and Experience sketched the architecture. The posts since then have sharpened individual joints. This post draws those joints together into one chain.

The starting point: no completed infinities

Everything begins with a refusal. Completed infinities — fully formed infinite totalities, given all at once — are rejected as a foundation. Not because infinity is large, but because a completed infinity is static. It offers no process, no construction, no step that follows from the previous one. Without that, causality dissolves and reliability vanishes. What remains is the potential infinite: the future, the ongoing, the open. That is enough to build on.

This refusal is not provable in a strict logical sense. As Finitism and the Origin shows, the origin of reality is illogical either way — a finite history requires emergence from nothing, an infinite one requires a completed series. The choice is therefore not between certainty and doubt, but between two models. The finite model concentrates the magic entirely at the origin and leaves the rest dependable. That dependability is what we need.

One rule, proportional action

A finite history implies a beginning, and a beginning implies a rule: something minimal from which everything else follows. This is the Wolfram hypothesis — not proven, but consistent with everything physics has found so far. One reliable rule, running on a hypergraph, generating space, time, and causality step by step.

The rule cannot be fully verified yet. But that does not paralyze action. It only calibrates it. The principle is simple: let the certainty of an action be proportional to its scope. With the knowledge we already have — quantum mechanics, general relativity, evolutionary biology — we can act reliably at human and civilizational scales. When and if the fundamental rule is found, the scope of responsible action expands accordingly. We do not wait for certainty. We act within its current reach.

Valence without a ground

Experience arises where causal structure becomes sufficiently integrated. IIT gives us the measure: Φ, the degree to which a system is more than the sum of its parts. Higher Φ means richer, more unified experience. But Φ alone carries no direction. It sets the depth of experience; it does not determine whether that experience is happiness or suffering.

That direction is entirely conditioning — Pavlovian, evolutionary, cultural, personal. There is no pre-conditional layer beneath it, no metaphysical ground that forces a sign onto experience. The ice cream is pleasant and the injury is painful not because pleasure and pain are written into the fabric of reality, but because billions of years of selection have shaped our conditioning in those directions.

This sounds like it could justify anything. But the opposite follows. From complete freedom, with no prior commitment, the choice for maximum happiness is not arbitrary — it is nearly tautological. Why choose less, when the choice is yours? And with an open future, maximum happiness means happiness without negative side effects: experiences that do not close off further experience, experiences that do not exhaust themselves.

The happiness singularity

The process this generates resembles what AI researchers call a self-improving system — a Darwin Gödel Machine that rewrites itself toward a goal. The mechanism is the same: complexity builds on complexity, each layer making the next possible. What changes is the fitness function.

Classical evolution optimizes for replication. Capitalism optimizes for accumulation. The AI singularity, left to itself, optimizes for capability. In this framework, the fitness function is happiness. Intelligence, complexity, and technology are means. The accumulation of rich, positive experience — deepening Φ, directed by conditioning toward things that never exhaust themselves — is the end.

The Whisper of the Ordinary maps the risk: habituation can serve this project or betray it. Adapting to suffering quietly is not progress. The relevant habituation is the gradual conditioning of ever-richer experience toward positive valence, without destroying the capacity for further growth.

Two domains, one project

The cathedral rests on a distinction that How Big is Big? approaches from the side of numbers. Reality has two faces. One is deterministic and knowable: the causal patterns that science can map, model, and eventually predict. Asteroids, viruses, psychoses — everything that threatens us is a pattern in the causal graph, never intrinsic malevolence. This domain is the domain of safety.

The other face is computationally irreducible. The last, most complex updates of reality cannot be simulated by any system inside reality. No shortcut exists. The only way to know how the music sounded is to have been in the room. This domain — irreducible, unsimulatable, always arriving for the first time — is the domain of happiness.

The two do not compete. Safety is the precondition for happiness, not its rival. Science conquers the deterministic domain not to replace experience but to protect it. The wider the safety, the more freely the irreducible can be lived.

This is the construction. Not a destination, but a direction: eliminate suffering through knowledge and cooperation, stack happiness through ever-deepening experience, let complexity build on complexity without a ceiling. The universe generates novelty without end. We learn, slowly, to receive it.

A word on the word

"Happiness" is a misleading label for what this framework describes. It sounds like contentment, like ease, like the absence of ambition. It is none of those things.

Health, creativity, knowledge, and talent are not alternatives to happiness here — they are happiness at higher Φ. A richer causal system experiences more deeply. The drive to understand, to create, to master is not separate from the pursuit of happiness; it is precisely the kind of inexhaustible activity that sustains positive valence without negative side effects. Choosing happiness over creativity is a false choice. At sufficient depth, they are the same thing.

As for the objection that one's employer, culture, or society optimizes for something else entirely: that is true, and the Cathedral addresses why — power structures optimize for self-preservation, not for the wellbeing of the people inside them. But this is not a reason for the individual to defer. The proportionality principle operates at every scale, including the personal. You do not need institutional permission to begin conditioning your own experience in a better direction. The project does not start with civilization. It starts wherever you are.
